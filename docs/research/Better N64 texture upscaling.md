# Route, pad and tune your way to better N64 upscales

R64texHD's biggest quality gains do not come from a new runtime. They come from three changes. The first is **running better-matched, bundle-safe texture models**: the CC-BY-4.0 Phhofm/Helaman game-texture models, the dither-aware `4xTextures_GTAV_rgt-s_dither` variant, CC0 `4x_HDCube` and the WTFPL 1x cleaners, in place of or alongside the photo-and-anime-trained Real-ESRGAN set. The second is **finishing the pipeline that pack makers already run by hand**: split alpha, bleed colour under it, re-threshold binary cut-outs, apply a low-frequency colour fix against the original, and, where it helps, upscale 4x then downscale. The third is **feeding the GPU properly**: whole-directory ncnn spawns with `-j 4:4:4`, an explicit GPU id and multi-GPU sharding, instead of small spawns of 32 files. A second runtime matters too, because the strongest permissively licensed models (DAT2, RGT, ATD) are transformers that ncnn is unlikely to run. **onnxruntime-node with the DirectML execution provider** is the one portable, Python-free way to run them on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs from Electron. One caution frames everything that follows: no modern model trained on N64 textures turned up. The only N64-specific model is the old, non-commercial `1x_N64clean`. The flagship community packs replaced their AI placeholders with hand-made art. So the app's edge lies in automation, sensible defaults and fast A/B comparison on the user's own textures, not in one magic model.

## Bundle CC-BY, CC0 and WTFPL models; ship UltraSharp only as an opt-in download

The licence picture is clean once you sort it. **4xTextureDAT2_otf** (DAT2, CC BY 4.0) is trained on 30,122 GTA V textures, handles JPEG damage, noise and slight blur, and is published as a **47.8 MB ONNX** file next to the .pth ([OpenModelDB](https://openmodeldb.info/models/4x-TextureDAT2-otf)). **4xTextures_GTAV_rgt-s** (RGT-S, CC BY 4.0) is the same author's game-texture model ([OpenModelDB](https://openmodeldb.info/models/4x-Textures-GTAV-rgt-s)). Its **_dither** variant (May 2024) adds dithering to the training data, which makes it the most directly relevant permissive model for dithered N64 art ([Phhofm/models](https://github.com/Phhofm/models)). **4x_HDCube** (ESRGAN, **CC0**) targets GameCube/Wii textures that suffered DXT and 8-bit colour compression and aims to keep "the original style". Its card warns that it is **not for pixel art, small icons or text under 16 px** ([OpenModelDB](https://openmodeldb.info/models/4x-HDCube)). The **Nomos2/Nomos8k DAT2, HAT and DRCT** models are also CC-BY-4.0 ([HF roundup](https://huggingface.co/easygoing0114/AI_upscalers)), as is the multipurpose **4xNomosUniDAT_otf** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Phips/4xNomosUniDAT_otf)). The legacy database adds WTFPL models that can be bundled freely: **4x_GameAI** (PS2-era textures), **4x_PixelPerfectV4**, **1x_DitherDeleterV3-Smooth**, **1x_DEDXT** and **1x_BCGone_Smooth** ([Upscale Wiki archive](https://gwern.net/doc/www/upscale.wiki/6a2f0435ebdeb3191d4e17408c5367cef41312d4.html)).

The popular names are the ones that are not safe to bundle. **4x-UltraSharp and 4x-UltraSharpV2 are CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0** ([OpenModelDB](https://openmodeldb.info/models/4x-UltraSharpV2)). So are 4x-FatePlus-lite, which "works best on dithered or quantized content", and the LyonHrt xBRZ/ScaleNx imitators. **1x_N64clean**, the one model built for N64's 5-bit-per-channel banding, is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([archive](https://gwern.net/doc/www/upscale.wiki/6a2f0435ebdeb3191d4e17408c5367cef41312d4.html)). ClearRealityV1 has conflicting licence statements, with NC in the README and Apache in the HF sidebar ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Kim2091/ClearRealityV1/commit/eb2802682b1ce5c7378f155c5d4d40d424652bee)). That alone is reason to treat it as NC. The **4xFSDedither** family is GPLv3. The "or later" clause allows R64texHD to ship it, but only if the combined distribution goes out under GPLv3. A non-commercial clause conflicts with GPL's freedom to use the program for any purpose. Model weights also carry their own licences, as OpenModelDB says plainly ([FAQ](https://openmodeldb.info/docs/faq)). The lowest-risk design is therefore a **model manager**. Bundled permissive models live in a `models/` folder, each with a credits/licence file, which CC-BY requires. NC models are fetched on demand behind a dialog that shows their licence. No legal source was found that addresses NC weights inside GPL apps, so keeping them as separate user-downloaded data files is the prudent reading.

Two caveats temper the table. First, every GTA V-trained model learned from far higher-resolution, photographic sources than a 32×32 4-bit texture. They will invent detail. That is an inference, not a measured result, and **no side-by-side comparison of these models on N64 textures exists**. The app should ship a built-in comparison grid rather than one hard-coded choice. Second, on the non-AI side, **MMPX is MIT** and is an easy bundle for HUD and font sprites. By its authors' own description it falls back to nearest-neighbour on anything that is not exact-colour pixel art ([mmpx README](https://rust-digger.code-maven.com/src/mmpx-0.2.0/README.md)). xBRZ ports are GPL-3.0 ([Maven xbrz-core](https://central.sonatype.com/artifact/io.github.stanio/xbrz-core)), with the same GPLv3 consequence as FSDedither. Rule-based scalers suit the Pixel lane of Smart mode, not world textures that the N64 bilinear-filters at render time.

## Tiny inputs make heavy transformer models affordable, but only through ONNX

The architecture ladder from traiNNer-redux (RTX 4090, 640×480 input, 4x) runs from **Compact at 227 fps and 0.2 GB VRAM**, through **ESRGAN at 4.7 fps and 2.48 GB**, to **DAT2 at 0.28 fps and 3.92 GB** ([traiNNer-redux benchmarks](https://trainner-redux.readthedocs.io/en/dev/benchmarks.html)). Over that range Urban100 PSNR rises from 26.18 dB (SPAN) to 27.86 dB (DAT2), and to 28.60 dB for HAT-L at 10.4 GB. Those numbers look prohibitive until you scale them to N64 sizes. A 64×64 texture has about **1/75 of the pixels** of a 640×480 frame, so DAT2 works out to very roughly 20 textures per second on a 4090. That is raw inference only, and mid-range GPUs are several times slower. Even so, a 5,000-texture dump stays in minutes-to-tens-of-minutes territory, so the app can afford "Quality" models. The cheap lanes, Compact, SPAN and RealPLKSR-tiny, are the only realistic choices for CPU-only and weak-iGPU machines. Phhofm trains the same datasets on several of these architectures, so a "Fast / Balanced / Quality" switch over one model family is easy to offer ([Phhofm/models](https://github.com/Phhofm/models)).

The catch is runtime support. The xinntao **realesrgan-ncnn-vulkan is MIT but frozen at v0.2.0**, and its promised model-conversion guide was never written ([repo](https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN-ncnn-vulkan)). The documented custom-model path is Upscayl's chaiNNer workflow, which converts .pth to .param/.bin. It requires **pinning chaiNNer v0.21.0** and hand-editing the input blob name from "input" to "data". It guarantees **only ESRGAN models**, and ONNX→ncnn "often fails" ([Upscayl wiki](https://github.com/upscayl/upscayl/wiki/Model-Conversion-Guide)). chaiNNer's own README says only ESRGAN and Waifu2x were tested on NCNN ([chaiNNer](https://github.com/chaiNNer-org/chaiNNer)). ncnn's official route, PyTorch → ONNX → onnx-simplifier → onnx2ncnn, chokes on Shape/Gather/Unsqueeze ops without simplification ([ncnn docs](https://ncnn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/how-to-use-and-FAQ/use-ncnn-with-pytorch-or-onnx.html)). The practical split follows from that. **ncnn-vulkan stays the engine for ESRGAN and Compact models**: HDCube, GameAI, PixelPerfect, the 1x cleaners and the existing Real-ESRGAN set. Convert these once at build time and ship them as .param/.bin. **DAT2 and RGT models go through ONNX.** upscayl-bin is the maintained ncnn fork (December 2025 build), but it is **AGPL-3.0** ([upscayl-ncnn](https://github.com/upscayl/upscayl-ncnn)). Keep the MIT xinntao or nihui binaries unless you deliberately move the app to GPLv3 terms.

## onnxruntime-node with DirectML is the second engine to add

**onnxruntime-node ships prebuilt CPU and DirectML providers for Windows x64 and arm64.** It is MIT-licensed, requires Electron ≥15 (28+ recommended) and needs no CUDA install. CUDA is prebuilt only for Linux ([ORT Node docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/get-started/with-javascript/node.html), [npm](https://npmjs.com/package/onnxruntime-node)). Microsoft's own Electron guide creates the session with `executionProviders: [{ name: 'dml', deviceId: 0 }, 'cpu']` and `graphOptimizationLevel: 'all'`. It runs inference in an Electron **`utilityProcess`** so the main process never blocks ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/ar-sa/windows/apps/dev-tools/winapp-cli/guides/electron-js-winml)). DirectML covers any DX12 GPU from NVIDIA Kepler, AMD GCN 1 and Intel Haswell onward, on Windows 10 1903+. It comes with constraints the integration must respect ([DirectML EP docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/DirectML-ExecutionProvider.html)). DirectML supports **ONNX opset ≤20**, so export at opset 17–20, and it lacks DeformConv and 5D GridSample. Memory-pattern optimisation and parallel execution must be off. **Only one thread may call `Run` per session**, so parallelism means one session per GPU or several sessions. Free-dimension overrides help with dynamic shapes, which argues for padding textures into fixed size buckets. The npm package's **218 MB unpacked size** spans every platform, and install pulls CUDA binaries by default. Pass `--onnxruntime-node-install-cuda=skip` and filter non-Windows binaries in electron-builder to keep the portable zip lean ([npm](https://npmjs.com/package/onnxruntime-node)).

Two strategic notes. DirectML is now in **"sustained engineering"**: it still works and still ships, but new development has moved to Windows ML ([DirectML EP docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/DirectML-ExecutionProvider.html)). Windows ML from Electron needs the Windows App SDK JS bindings, version-locked onnxruntime-node 1.24.x, on-demand EP downloads via `ensureReadyAsync()`, and a flow built around debug identity and MSIX ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/ar-sa/windows/apps/dev-tools/winapp-cli/guides/electron-js-winml)). That conflicts with "portable and offline", so DirectML is the right default today and WinML is a later, optional path. The heavier alternatives are worse fits. TensorRT and CUDA backends each need **over 1 GB of CUDA libraries**, and TensorRT engines must be built on the target machine ([vs-mlrt](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2FAmusementClub%2Fvs-mlrt)), which makes TensorRT at most an optional "NVIDIA turbo" download. chaiNNer is GPL-3.0 and downloads its own Python ([chaiNNer](https://github.com/chaiNNer-org/chaiNNer)), so it breaks the no-Python constraint. A WebGPU plugin EP for ORT exists (v0.5.0, October), but whether onnxruntime-node can load it is unconfirmed ([ORT releases](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/releases)). Real gaps remain. **No head-to-head benchmark of ncnn-vulkan against ORT-DirectML** on the same GPU was found; vs-mlrt only calls ncnn "the slowest option" with "a much smaller footprint". fp16 behaviour on DirectML and DAT/HAT export failures are also unverified. Budget a spike that exports TextureDAT2 and RGT-S, checks DirectML compatibility and times both engines on an AMD and an Intel card.

## Alpha, seams and colour drift are where automation beats raw model quality

The app already pads by 8 px using RT64 wrap/mirror info and has a hard-alpha option. That puts it ahead of most tools. The remaining wins are well documented. The standard alpha practice is **split, bleed, upscale separately, re-quantise**. RunDevelopment's DS3TexUp pipeline (he is a chaiNNer developer) extends colours into transparent areas, upscales RGB and greyscale alpha as separate images, and "quantize[s] the upscaled 8-bit alpha back to binary" for cut-outs ([DS3TexUp](https://github.com/RunDevelopment/DS3TexUp/blob/main/Upscaling.md)). Edge padding exists because filtering otherwise blends edge colour with the black RGB under transparent pixels and draws dark fringes ([Hargreaves](https://www.shawnhargreaves.com/blog/texture-filtering-alpha-cutouts.html)). Key-colour transparency is worse still: the GAN treats the key colour as content and bleeds it into edges ([ut_crispifier](https://github.com/butterwagon69/ut_crispifier)). joeyballentine's ESRGAN offered `--binary-alpha --alpha-threshold .2` and a ternary mode with a boundary offset ([esrgan](https://github.com/joeyballentine/esrgan)). That shows the threshold is a tunable, not a constant. One chaiNNer user found colour-fix nodes break RGBA textures unless alpha is split first ([chaiNNer #2456](https://github.com/chaiNNer-org/chaiNNer/issues/2456)). Every RGB post-process should therefore run before alpha is re-merged.

For R64texHD, alpha should be classified per texture as opaque, binary or graded. RT64 dumps carry raw TMEM and tile data ([RT64 TEXTURE-PACKS.md](https://github.com/rt64/rt64/blob/main/TEXTURE-PACKS.md)), so the native format is knowable. An RGBA5551 source is binary by construction, and IA4/IA8 are intensity plus alpha. Under that scheme, binary sources get a dilation-bled RGB, a separately upscaled alpha (model or bicubic) and a threshold around 0.5 that the user can adjust. Graded sources keep model-upscaled alpha. IA textures are upscaled as greyscale to stop hue drift. This classification is an inference from the format, not a sourced practice, but it is cheap and testable. It also feeds BC7 mip generation (see the next section). GLideN64 packs need the emulator's "full alpha channel" option for smooth alpha ([SM64-Reloaded](https://github.com/GhostlyDark/SM64-Reloaded)). That is a reminder that graded alpha is real, intended content, not noise to threshold away.

On seams, the established `--seamless` modes are **tile (wrap), mirror, replicate and alpha_pad** ([esrgan](https://github.com/joeyballentine/esrgan)). R64texHD has the first two. It should add replicate for clamp-mode tiles. For textures of 32×32 or smaller, the pad should grow from a fixed 8 px to a full 3×3 tiling of the texture, cropping the centre afterwards. An 8 px margin is a large fraction of a 16 px texture and may sit inside the model's receptive field. No source gives architecture-specific pad widths, so validate this empirically. Seam handling must also continue past the upscale: **texconv defaults to clamp addressing for resize and mip filtering**, and exposes `-wrap`/`-mirror` ([texconv wiki](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectXTex/wiki/texconv)). cdlewis's RT64 packer also mips with "clamped edges" ([texture-packer](https://github.com/cdlewis/texture-packer)). Repeating textures mipped with clamp develop visible seams at distance even when mip 0 is perfect, so the same wrap/mirror flag should reach texconv. RT64's default "half" texel shift and the manual tile shifting in MK64 Reloaded ([MK64 Reloaded](https://evilgames.eu/texture-packs/mk64-reloaded.htm)) suggest some alignment errors are a game-data problem, not an upscaling one.

Pre- and post-processing is where sources disagree, and the disagreement is informative. GDCorner denoised before ESRGAN because "dithering noise is a particular problem", then **halved the 4x output** to suppress artefacts ([GDCorner](https://www.gdcorner.com/2019/11/25/TextureUpscalingPipeline.html)). DS3TexUp warns that denoise and de-BC models "remove high-frequency detail, leaving flat results" ([DS3TexUp](https://github.com/RunDevelopment/DS3TexUp/blob/main/Upscaling.md)). The resolution is to make the 1x pre-pass **conditional**. Run DitherDeleterV3-Smooth only when a detector finds ordered-dither signatures, such as repeated 2×2 checker deltas. Run DEDXT/BCGone only on inputs that are already BC-compressed DDS. Or skip the pre-pass and use the dither-trained RGT-S model, which folds cleanup into the upscale. Model chaining with a 1x cleaner followed by a 4x model is a long-standing pattern ([esrgan](https://github.com/joeyballentine/esrgan)), and ncnn's per-folder spawns make it cheap. On the output side, three automatable techniques recur. First, an **average colour fix against the original**, which chaiNNer ships as Average Color Fix, applied to RGB only. Implement it as `result += upsample(original − downsample(result))` at low frequency, which pins colours to the source without touching detail. Second, **"render at 4x, deliver at 2x"** with a proper Lanczos or Mitchell downscale. texconv offers only point, linear, cubic, fant, box and triangle ([texconv wiki](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectXTex/wiki/texconv)), so do the resize in the app's own image code. Third, **multi-model ensembles with automatic selection**. DS3TexUp runs UltraSharp alone and as a 50/50 blend, then keeps whichever scores sharper, while noting that the blend fails on blurry inputs ([DS3TexUp](https://github.com/RunDevelopment/DS3TexUp/blob/main/Upscaling.md)). The scale target should match how players render. Dolphin's guidance ties texture scale to internal resolution ([dvessel gist](https://gist.github.com/dvessel/4884272446e68956740aef261b54d3c1)), and the Reloaded packs ship downscaled HD variants alongside 4K ones ([MK64 Reloaded](https://evilgames.eu/texture-packs/mk64-reloaded.htm)).

## BC7_UNORM with full mips, coverage and wrap-aware filtering is the right output

RT64's own guidance is unambiguous. It wants DDS, ideally BC7, which cuts VRAM almost 4x compared with PNG. It says **"Do not ship texture packs using PNG files to end users."** It recommends embedded mipmaps for anisotropic filtering, a **low-mip cache of about 100–200 MB** to avoid pop-in, and a zstd `.rtz`. It also warns that **Compressonator's GPU encoder loses colour precision** ("full white may not map to 1.0") ([RT64 TEXTURE-PACKS.md](https://github.com/rt64/rt64/blob/main/TEXTURE-PACKS.md)). cdlewis's packer writes **BC7_UNORM, not _SRGB, "to match RT64's sampling"**, with sRGB-aware mip filtering ([texture-packer](https://github.com/cdlewis/texture-packer)). The texconv switches that matter are `-bc x` (maximum BC7 quality, enabling modes 0 and 2, slow), `-bc q` (mode 6 only, fast), `-m 0` (full chain), `-nogpu`, `-dx10`, `--keep-coverage <ref>`, `-sepalpha`, `-srgbi/-srgbo` and `-wrap/-mirror` ([texconv wiki](https://github.com/microsoft/DirectXTex/wiki/texconv)).

A good default for R64texHD is therefore `texconv -f BC7_UNORM -bc x -m 0 -dx10 -y`. Add `--keep-coverage <t>` only for binary-alpha textures, using the same threshold as the re-thresholding step. Add `-wrap` or `-mirror` from the RT64 tile flags. Use `-sepalpha` where alpha is not transparency. Offer `-bc q` as a "fast export" setting, because `-bc x` on thousands of textures is CPU-bound. That makes it a natural candidate for the app's existing worker-thread pool: run several texconv processes in parallel, one per core minus the GPU feeder. Keep HUD, font and flat-colour UI textures at R8G8B8A8_UNORM, or at least `-bc x`, since block artefacts show worst on flat colour ([dvessel gist](https://gist.github.com/dvessel/4884272446e68956740aef261b54d3c1)). One item needs verification before shipping: whether `-srgbi` with a _UNORM target gives gamma-correct mip filtering without converting the stored data. If texconv converts, generate the mip levels in linear light inside the app, or leave sRGB flags off. No source confirms how RT64 treats BC7_UNORM_SRGB.

## Throughput comes from fewer spawns, explicit GPUs and right-sized tiles

The ncnn tools document exactly the knobs the app needs ([Real-ESRGAN-ncnn-vulkan](https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN-ncnn-vulkan), [waifu2x-ncnn-vulkan](https://github.com/nihui/waifu2x-ncnn-vulkan)). `-i`/`-o` accept directories. `-g 0,1,2` spreads work over several GPUs, with per-GPU `-t` and `-j` lists such as `-j 1:2,2,2:2`. The README advice is **`-j 4:4:4` for many small images**, against a default of 1:2:2. Batches of 32 per spawn pay model load and Vulkan initialisation on every batch. For a 5,000-texture dump that is about 156 cold starts. The better pattern is to hard-link or copy each lane's textures (already padded, already routed by Smart mode) into one staging folder per model and per GPU. Then spawn **once per folder** with `-j 4:4:4` or higher load/save counts, and stream progress by watching the output folder. Always pass `-g` explicitly, since NCNN can default to the integrated GPU ([chaiNNer](https://github.com/chaiNNer-org/chaiNNer)). Enumerate GPUs and let the user tick which ones to use. Split work across GPUs by total pixel count, not file count.

Tiling barely matters at N64 sizes, but it is easy to get wrong in both directions. In nihui's GTX 1070 measurements, a **200×200 image used 638 MB at tile 200 or 400 but only 197 MB at tile 100**, and 2000×2000 dropped from 2430 MB to 213 MB ([waifu2x-ncnn-vulkan](https://github.com/nihui/waifu2x-ncnn-vulkan)). A padded 256 px texture fits comfortably in one tile on any modern card. Setting `-t` at least as large as the padded input avoids internal tile seams entirely and is free in VRAM terms. Reserve smaller tiles for low-VRAM iGPUs, where you can raise `-j` instead. On the ORT side, the levers differ. Keep one long-lived session per GPU in a utilityProcess, which avoids reload cost entirely. Bucket textures by padded size (32/64/128/256+pad), batch same-size items along N, and use free-dimension overrides. Run several sessions only if DirectML's one-`Run`-per-session rule leaves the GPU idle ([DirectML EP docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/DirectML-ExecutionProvider.html)). **Atlasing**, packing many tiny textures into one large padded image, is the other way to amortise per-call overhead. It needs each texture's own wrap/mirror pad inside the atlas to prevent bleed, and **no source measured its gain** over directory batching. Treat it as an experiment for the ORT path, not a default. For CPU-only users, `-g -1` CPU fallback is documented for the nihui tools but not confirmed for realesrgan-ncnn-vulkan. A Compact or SPAN ONNX model on ORT's CPU provider, one session per worker, is the more predictable CPU lane. No source quantified CPU throughput.

## Prioritised roadmap for R64texHD

Items 1–6 need no new dependencies and touch code the app already has: the spawn wrapper, the texconv call and the padding step. Items 7–10 extend the existing ncnn path and Smart mode. Item 11 is the one real architectural addition. It should be built in parallel with an early spike that confirms TextureDAT2 and RGT-S export at opset ≤20 and run correctly on DirectML before you commit to bundling them.

## Conclusion

The research turns the usual framing upside down. "Better upscaling" for N64 textures is less about finding a stronger network than about **not damaging what the network gets right**. That means bleeding colour under cut-outs, keeping seams wrapped through mip generation, pinning colours to the original palette and choosing whether to dedither per texture. Each of these is cheap, deterministic and fully under R64texHD's control. Meanwhile the field offers no model trained on N64 data, and the best community packs still finish by hand. The implication is that R64texHD's lasting advantage is **knowing each texture's native format and tiling from the RT64 dump**, which no general-purpose upscaler has, and using that metadata to drive every step from padding to `--keep-coverage`.

On compute, the tiny size of N64 textures removes the usual trade-off: heavy transformer models become affordable as soon as they are reachable. That makes a DirectML ONNX engine strategically more important than any ncnn tuning, even though ncnn tuning is the faster win. DirectML's "sustained engineering" status is the main forward risk. Keeping the ONNX integration behind a thin provider abstraction would let the app move to WebGPU or Windows ML later without reworking the pipeline.
